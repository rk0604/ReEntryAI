# JEPA world model + MPPI for Orion entry guidance — plan

Inspired by Rao, Zhang, Balestriero, LeCun, Loianno, *SkyJEPA: Learning
Long-Horizon World Models for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Control of Quadrotors*
(arXiv 2606.23444, 2026). Code: `jepa/`. Experiment: `ReEntryAI_JEPA.ipynb`
(generated by `jepa/build_notebook.py`).

---

## 1. Why this, and the hypothesis

Every learned controller so far has fallen well short of the classical
predictor-corrector (2.05 km median miss, 99.9% success on the frozen
1,000-case test set):

| Approach | Failure mode |
|---|---|
| PPO from scratch | the reversal schedule is never discovered by exploration (~1% success) |
| Behavior clone | compounding error off the expert's state distribution; 91 km median |
| Clone → PPO | cold critic destroys the clone; 438 km |

All three learn a **policy**. SkyJEPA instead learns a **model** and plans
through it with MPPI. That sidesteps both failure modes. The model is learned from any
data by self-supervision, not from reward, so there is no exploration problem. Feedback
comes from re-planning every few seconds, not from a policy that drifts off
distribution. Our classical controller is itself a model-based planner (constant-bank rollouts of
a nominal model), so this swaps its hand-built predictor for a learned one.

**Hypothesis.** A JEPA latent world model trained only on randomized
closed-loop sim data, with a physics-inspired prober and MPPI:

1. predicts the drogue-deploy footprint over long horizons better than an
   autoregressive state-space model of similar size (SkyJEPA's central claim,
   transplanted to a 300–500 s horizon instead of 1 s);
2. lands within the 25 km success radius on most frozen test cases, which beats
   every learned controller so far;
3. degrades **less** than the classical controller when the true density and
   L/D differ from nominal. The classical predictor uses a fixed nominal model,
   while the world model can infer the truth from 35 s of history (implicit system ID).

Matching the 2 km classical bar outright is a stretch goal, not the hypothesis.

## 2. SkyJEPA → ReEntryAI mapping

| SkyJEPA | ReEntryAI | Note |

| state p, v, R, ω at 20 Hz | h, φ, λ, V, γ, χ, realized σ, roll rate, sensed drag/lift accel, at 5 s macro steps | λ dropped from encoder input (dynamics are λ-invariant); sensed accel = IMU |
| action: 4 rotor forces | 1 bank command, held 5 s | same handle and ±90° limit as the RL env |
| H = 10 (0.5 s) history | rf = 7 macro steps (35 s) | causal TCN receptive field |

| L_pred + 0.02·SIGReg, 17 knots | same | SIGReg pooled over time, 1024 rows × 32 projections (cost) |

| MPPI tracking cost | MPPI on predicted miss at drogue + smoothness + g + γ constraint | §3.6 |

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Data synthesis (`jepa/data.py`)
* **Excitation.** 50% GP bank profiles (σ = 90°·tanh(sum of RBF GPs with 15 s,
  50 s, 150 s length scales + bias)), 50% reversal-structured profiles (slowly
  varying magnitude 5–90°, Poisson sign flips every ~60–200 s). Commands are
  held per 5 s macro step, exactly as MPPI issues them.
* **Feasibility.** Each profile is flown by `OrionEntryEnv` in policy mode, so the
  bank actuator (20 °/s, 40 °/s²), roll PD, RCS and 3DOF EOM are all in the loop.
  The episode ends at drogue deploy, at the corridor gate, or at a 900 s cap.
* **Robustness.** The truth is perturbed through existing params knobs, so no
  physics module changes: `ref_area_m2` (q·S scales exactly like density for
  forces), `cpas_lift_scale` (CL, inactive above drogue), `mass_kg`. The
  classical guidance holds its own params copy, so the same mutation creates a
  model mismatch for it.
* **Logging.** 1 Hz, then subsampled to macro steps, so Δt can change without
  recollection. Macro-mean specific forces are kept as the prober's auxiliary target.
* **Budget.** 320 episodes ≈ 32k macro transitions (FULL), about 8 s of sim per episode.

### 3.2 World model (`jepa/models.py`)
`s_t = Enc_θ(X_t)`, `z_t = Enc_φ(A_t)`, `s̃_{t+1} = readout(GRU(z_t, h_t))`,
with h₀ = tanh(W s_t). The latent is a linear readout of the GRU state, so it can
reach the unbounded N(0, I) targets. Both encoders are valid-padding causal
TCNs, so one pass over a window yields every sliding encoding (all T+1 targets,
or all planned action codes) at once.

Loss: `mean‖s̃_{t+k} − s_{t+k}‖² + λ·SIGReg`, with λ = 0.02. SIGReg is the
Epps-Pulley characteristic-function test on random 1-D projections. Targets come
from the same encoder with gradients (LeJEPA style: no EMA, no stop-gradient, no decoder).

### 3.3 Why the prober has a nominal model in it
The first smoke test made this choice for us. The **nominal-physics footprint
predictor** (the sim's own NRLMSISE table + Orion aero schedule at nominal
parameters, instantaneous bank) already predicts the drogue point to ~0.6 km
from 100 s out and ~23 km from 300 s out (FULL run, median), even under the
randomized truth. A prober that learns all aero forces from scratch cannot get
near that. The JEPA+MLP ablation confirms it: 98 km at 300 s.

### 3.4 Physics-inspired prober
Per macro step the frozen latent s̃ maps to three corrections (c_D, c_L, Δσ) of a deliberately crude prior:

```
a_D  = ½ ρ0 e^{−h/7.2 km} V² / β_nom · e^{c_D}        (one exponential atmosphere, β from nominal CD = 1.5)
a_L  = a_D · 0.30 · e^{c_L}
σ_eff = σ_cmd + 90°·tanh(Δσ)                            (actuator lag; the paper's K_t a_t is also action-linear)
```

The forces are evaluated at the **integrated** h and V every Euler substep (2 per
macro step), then go through the exact 3DOF EOM of `math_3d.nominal_eom_step`.
Drag therefore responds to the rollout's own altitude, which stabilizes long
rollouts. This is the analog of the paper's nominal thrust term using the
integrated attitude. The latent has to carry the real density profile, the
Mach-scheduled CD and L/D, the actuator lag and the randomized truth. The prober
is zero-initialized, so before training it *is* the crude prior. That gives a free
ablation, "physics prior only".

The loss is the paper's supervised rollout loss on dimensionless state errors
(1 km altitude, 5 km position, 50 m/s, 0.5°, 0.5°), plus an auxiliary match to
the sensed macro-mean forces. The auxiliary term is not in the paper; it keeps the
g-load the planner reads physical.

### 3.5 Baselines and ablations
| Name | What it isolates |
|---|---|
| Predictive | autoregressive state-space model, same encoders, one-step teacher-forced (paper's "Predictive") |
| JEPA + MLP prober | physics structure in the decoder (paper's "Ours + Prober"); free-form per-step increments |
| Physics prior only | does the latent add information beyond the crude prior? |
| physics-nominal (open loop) | the classical predictor's model: high-fidelity, nominal params, instant bank |
| Classical PC (closed loop) | the bar; its predictor keeps the nominal model under mismatch |
| MPPI on Predictive (closed loop) | paper's closed-loop comparison |

### 3.6 MPPI (`jepa/mppi.py`)
* One decision per 5 s. 256 samples (FULL), horizon ≤ 110 macro steps. The rollout
  stops once every sample is below drogue altitude.
* The noise is on 6 knots spread over the *predicted* time-to-go, linearly
  interpolated (σ = 30°). The effective dimension stays small while the horizon shrinks.
* Cost, in km-equivalents: predicted miss at the drogue crossing (interpolated)
  + 2 km/rad of command change + 50·(g − 8)² + 2·(|γ| − 82°)² before
  drogue + 3000 km if the sample never reaches drogue.
* Temperature = 0.5 × (10th-percentile cost − min cost). This makes MPPI scale-free
  between a 500 km early miss and a 2 km late one.
* Warm start shifted by one step. 4 iterations at entry interface, 1 afterwards.
* No sign logic or deadband: reversals come out of the cost.

**Smoke-test finding (fixed):** without the γ term, MPPI flew the terminal phase
at σ ≈ 90°, so γ reached −87° at 8.4 km and the env's corridor gate ended the
flight just above drogue. The classical controller avoids this with a hard rule
(wings level once |γ| > 30°). The classical controller reaches drogue at
γ ≈ −79°, so the soft limit sits at 82°. This is the paper's interpretability
argument in miniature: the PI prober exposes γ, so the constraint is one line.

### 3.7 Evaluation protocol
**Open loop** on held-out episodes (all with randomized truth), with the true future commands:
* RMSE of position / altitude / speed / γ vs. unroll, to 2.5× the training
  horizon, plus error growth per step (paper Eq. 30). The paper's compounding
  ratio is not used: its teacher-forced denominator is not well defined for a
  latent model.
* **Footprint error vs. time-to-go** (400 … 50 s), split by how far the truth
  was from nominal. This is the guidance-relevant metric.
* Latent health (per-dim std, effective rank) and temporal straightening.

**Closed loop** on the first N frozen test-set ICs (`configs/heldout_testset.csv`,
replayed by value):
* truths: *nominal*, and *mismatch* (density ×0.88, L/D ×0.88, inside the training range);
* controllers: JEPA-MPPI, Predictive-MPPI, classical PC;
* metrics: success (< 25 km), miss median / p90, peak g, RCS fuel, reversals,
  planning ms per decision.

## 4. Compute budget (measured on this laptop: 12 logical cores, CPU-only torch)

| Stage | QUICK (measured) | FULL (measured) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data | 24 eps, 77 s | 296 new eps, 312 s | ~8 s of sim per episode; ~6× effective parallelism; cache is extended, not rebuilt |
| JEPA (stage 1) | 300 steps, 11 s | 3000 steps, 170 s | B = 256, T = 24 |
| Probers (stage 2) | 11 s | 108 s | PI prober T = 16 |
| Predictive | 4 s | 53 s | |
| Open-loop eval | ~5 s | ~15 s | |
| Closed loop | 12 eps, 152 s | 92 eps, 695 s | classical cached across runs (4 reused) |
| **Total** | **4 min 40 s** | **22 min 32 s** | 12-core laptop, CPU only |

Profiling notes that set the defaults:
* **One torch thread is fastest.** The models are op-dispatch bound: a JEPA step is
  35 ms on 1 thread and 90–235 ms on 4–12 threads.
* SIGReg at 2048 rows × 64 projections was ~40 ms of a ~75 ms step. Cutting it to
  1024 × 32 costs ~5 ms.
* The prober's cost is thousands of tiny elementwise ops in the integrator. A
  shorter prober horizon (16 vs 24) is the cheap lever; `torch.compile` is not
  available on this Windows box.
* **Planning costs ~40–60 ms per 5 s decision** on one thread (~10 ms per second of
  flight). The classical predictor-corrector costs ~124 ms per 1 s step, so this is
  ~10–15× cheaper per second of flight. Classical episodes are ~70 s alone and
  ~140 s under full-machine contention.

## 5. Phases

**Phase 1 — offline model + first head-to-head (this notebook).** Data,
stage 1/2, baselines, open-loop metrics, closed loop on 16 cases × 2 truths.
*Exit:* §6 criteria evaluated, and a go/no-go on Phase 2.

**Phase 2 — close the gap to classical** (only if Phase 1 is a go):
1. *On-policy data (the paper's NMPC/MPPI tracking data, DAgger-like).* Fly
   JEPA-MPPI with exploration noise across dispersed ICs and truths, add those
   episodes, and fine-tune. Random profiles rarely visit the near-target terminal
   states the planner actually lives in. Cheap: ~25 s per episode.
2. *Terminal phase.* Replan at 1 Hz once time-to-go < 60 s, and consider a 2 s
   macro step for the last minute (the 5 s hold quantizes the final correction).
3. *Horizon.* Curriculum T = 24 → 48, and per-step SIGReg as in the paper.
   Check whether the error growth beyond the training horizon flattens.
4. *Scale.* 1,000 episodes is still < 30 min of collection. Report the full
   1,000-case test set (one ~2 h overnight classical run, cached forever).

**Phase 3 — safety (ties into the paper contribution already in the repo).**
* MPPI proposes and the existing interval shield (`OrionEntryEnv(interval_shield=True)`)
  certifies. Report shield intervention rate as a measure of how often the learned
  planner proposes something the guaranteed bounds reject.
* Heat-rate term in the MPPI cost (Sutton-Graves from the prober's h, V).
  It is not binding in Scenario B, but it is needed for Scenario A.
* Ensemble of 3–5 probers or world models. Use disagreement as an
  epistemic-uncertainty penalty in the MPPI cost.

**Phase 4 — stress.** Out-of-range truths (density ×0.75, L/D ×0.8), Scenario A
lunar return (a different dynamics regime, to test zero-shot transfer of a
model trained on B), and a classical baseline *with* a density/L-D filter (a fairer
opponent for claim 3).

## 6. Decision criteria (fixed before looking at FULL results)

| Criterion | Go if |
|---|---|
| No collapse | latent std ≈ 1 on most dims, effective rank > 8 of 32 |
| Latent adds information | JEPA+PI footprint median < Physics-prior-only at τ = 200 s and 300 s |
| Paper's claim holds here | JEPA+PI footprint median < Predictive at τ ≥ 200 s |
| Usable for guidance | JEPA-MPPI success ≥ 75% on nominal truth |
| Robustness story | JEPA-MPPI median miss grows less than classical's from nominal → mismatch |

Phase 2 is worth it if the first four hold. Claim 3 is worth writing up only if the
fifth also holds, *and* it survives Phase 4's fairer classical baseline.

## 7. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Long-horizon drift (4.6× training horizon) | prober recomputes drag from the integrated state; replanning every 5 s; Phase 2 curriculum |
| Planner exploits model errors (adversarial optimum) | soft constraints on interpretable state; ensemble disagreement penalty (Phase 3); on-policy data (Phase 2) |
| Random-profile data misses the near-target terminal regime | on-policy data (Phase 2.1) |
| Unfair comparison | same IC values, same truth, same actuator chain; the classical controller has no density estimator (stated, and fixed in Phase 4); both assume perfect navigation; JEPA uses sensed specific force (IMU), which real entry guidance also uses |
| SIGReg weight mis-set (collapse or unpredictable latents) | latent health printed after stage 1; λ sweep {0.005, 0.02, 0.05} is ~3 min each |

## 8. Deviations from the paper (on purpose)
* The prober corrects a nominal aero model (multiplicative corrections plus a bank
  offset) rather than adding a residual acceleration, and it uses the command.
  Reason: §3.3.
* SIGReg is pooled over rollout steps and subsampled (cost). An auxiliary
  force loss is added in the prober stage.
* The MLP prober emits per-step increments, not absolute state, because the encoder
  never sees longitude.
* MPPI uses knot-interpolated noise and an adaptive temperature, because the task is
  terminal targeting over ~100 steps, not tracking over 15.

## 9. First results (FULL run, 2026-10-01, one seed)

The executed notebook is saved at `jepa_runs/full/ReEntryAI_JEPA_full_executed.ipynb`.

**Closed loop** (first 16 frozen test-set ICs, same IC values and truth for every controller):

| Truth | Controller | Success (< 25 km) | Median miss | p90 miss | Peak g (mean) | RCS fuel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominal | classical PC | 100% | 2.24 km | 6.9 km | 3.67 | 87 kg |
| nominal | **JEPA-MPPI** | **100%** | **1.69 km** | **4.7 km** | 5.61 | 36 kg |
| nominal | Predictive-MPPI | 87.5% | 13.1 km | 34.1 km | 5.47 | 40 kg |
| mismatch | classical PC | 87.5% | 3.85 km | 23.8 km | 3.88 | 58 kg |
| mismatch | **JEPA-MPPI** | **93.8%** | 5.07 km | **11.7 km** | 4.63 | 36 kg |
| mismatch | Predictive-MPPI | 81.3% | 10.2 km | 45.0 km | 4.82 | 40 kg |

The classical controller's 16-case nominal median (2.24 km) matches its 1,000-case figure
(2.05 km), so the subset is representative. Planning cost was 316–332 ms per 5 s
decision under 11-worker contention (~45 ms on an idle core).

**Open loop** (45 held-out randomized-truth episodes, median footprint error):

| Time-to-go | JEPA+PI | Physics prior only | Predictive | JEPA+MLP | physics-nominal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400 s | 40.5 km | 57.2 | 53.8 | 159 | 32.3 |
| 300 s | **19.7** | 36.0 | 27.1 | 98 | 22.6 |
| 200 s | 6.0 | 11.3 | 9.3 | 42 | 5.7 |
| 100 s | 1.1 | 0.9 | 4.4 | 18.5 | 0.6 |

Latent: effective rank 14.6 / 32, per-dim std 0.75–1.6, val latent MSE 0.068. Straightening:
rollout latents 0.90, encoded true latents 0.69, raw state features −0.29. That is the paper's
"latent trajectories are straighter" finding.

**Decision criteria (§6):**

| Criterion | Result |
|---|---|
| No collapse | **met**: effective rank 14.6 > 8 |
| Latent adds information | **met**: 6.0 vs 11.3 km at 200 s, 19.7 vs 36.0 km at 300 s |
| Paper's claim holds here | **met**: JEPA+PI beats Predictive at every τ ≥ 200 s, and in closed loop (1.7 vs 13.1 km) |
| Usable for guidance | **met**: 100% success on nominal truth (16/16) |
| Robustness story | **not met as written**: the median grows 1.69 → 5.07 km for JEPA and 2.24 → 3.85 km for classical. The tail points the other way (p90 4.7 → 11.7 vs 6.9 → 23.8; success 100 → 94% vs 100 → 88%). In open loop JEPA+PI only ties the high-fidelity nominal model on far-from-nominal truths, so there is no evidence yet of history-based system ID. |

**Verdict: go for Phase 2.** Before anyone reads "beats classical" into this, three caveats:
1. **The comparison is not g-matched.** JEPA-MPPI averages 5.6 g peak against 3.7 g for
   the classical controller, because the cost only penalizes g above 8. It may be buying miss distance with
   load. **First task in Phase 2:** add a g-preference term (or a 4.5 g soft limit) and re-run.
2. **n = 16, one seed.** A 1.69 vs 2.24 km median difference is inside the noise at this
   size. Scale to ≥ 100 cases. The classical side is a one-off ~40 min and is cached.
3. Inspect JEPA-MPPI's one mismatch failure next to the classical controller's two (cases 5 and 14,
   90 km and 25 km) and its 10–25 km cases (2, 4, 9, 11). Is it the same cases? Is it the terminal phase?
